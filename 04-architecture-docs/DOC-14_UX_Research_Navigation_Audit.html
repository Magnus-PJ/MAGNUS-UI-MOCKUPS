DOC-14 — World-Class Healthcare UX Research & Navigation Audit

Deep research on leading global & Indian platforms (navigation, single-page IA, click efficiency, search, AI), audited against the 232-screen pack, with the prioritised improvement backlog UX-1…UX-10. Version 1.0 · 26 July 2026.

Executive summaryThe world's best healthcare UX converges on five ideas: one-page patient context with progressive disclosure, task-inbox-first navigation, a global role-scoped search/command layer, measured click budgets, and zero-scroll decision screens. MagnusPRO is already at or ahead of par on worklists, the radiology single-pane loop, safety gating and responsive design — but the audit confirms your observation: search exists on only 8 of 232 screens, there is no global omni-search, no unified task inbox, no recents, and 12+ screens are scroll-heavy. Closing UX-1…UX-10 below is what separates a very good HMS from a world-class one — and in the Indian market, where incumbent UX is weak, it is the differentiator.

2 · The navigation & IA patterns that define world-class
#World-class patternDefinition / evidence
P1Patient-360 one page + progressive disclosureEverything about the patient on one page as summary cards; details open as overlay panels/drawers (not new pages) — Epic Storyboard, Cerner MPages. Sub-pages only for legally distinct acts (sign, consent, pay)
P2Persistent context headerPatient/visit identity + alerts pinned on every screen in a flow; eliminates wrong-patient errors and re-orientation time
P3Global omni-search / command palette (Ctrl+K)One box that finds patients, orders, invoices, reports, screens and ACTIONS, scoped to the user's role & purpose; modern SaaS standard, appearing in healthcare (Ottehr et al.)
P4Task-inbox-first navigationUsers open 'what needs me' (worklists/queues with counts), not module menus — athenaOne model; our worklists exist but there is no single 'My tasks' aggregation
P5Click budgetsEvidence: ED physicians ≈4,000 clicks/shift (Hill et al.); KLAS Arch Collaborative case studies target click reduction as the #1 efficiency lever. Rule: top-20 tasks each get a click budget (e.g., register→token ≤12, sign routine report ≤5, collect cash ≤6) and are tested against it
P6Zero-scroll above-the-fold for decisionsPrimary decision + primary action visible without scrolling at 1366×768; long content = tabs/accordions/drawers, sticky action bar at bottom
P7Keyboard-first for high-volume rolesReading, billing, registration all fully keyboard-drivable; shortcuts discoverable in the palette
P8Recents & favouritesLast 5 patients/studies/invoices one click away per user; removes the most common search entirely
P9Every list has find-as-you-typeNo table without an instant filter; server search for cross-record queries, purpose-logged
P10NLQ / 'ask the record'Emerging: natural-language questions over the chart (Oracle CDA, Epic AI charting) — roadmap hook, not MVP
3 · Click-budget evidence

The seminal ED study measured ≈4,000 clicks per 10-hour shift (Hill et al., AJEM 2013); KLAS Arch Collaborative case studies repeatedly show click-reduction programs as the highest-yield EHR-experience lever, and its 2025 physician guidebook ties satisfaction primarily to efficiency-of-navigation, not features. Conclusion: adopt explicit budgets per task and test them (UX-8).
